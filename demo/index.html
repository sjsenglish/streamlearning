Question 2 · 빈칸 추론
다음 빈칸에 들어갈 말로 가장 적절한 것은?
Assessments of which buildings deserve to endure have shifted in every generation. Monuments one era protects, a later era dismisses as imitation, and structures once condemned as eyesores enter the canon* of architecture. Whatever merit a building is granted rests, at best, on a present-day consensus, and every consensus of this kind has in time given way to another. Members of preservation boards know this instability well, whether they rule on a private petition or a public landmark. To list a building is to claim for it a place in architectural history, or to claim nothing beyond shelter and sentiment. An approved listing asserts a belief about the future: that the building will hold a significant place in architecture as a whole. No present consensus can underwrite** such an assertion; the listing is, in the end, the board's own act. Through it the members affirm not only their architectural judgment, from whatever training it derives, but the conviction that ____________.
* canon: 정전(공인된 목록)    ** underwrite: 보증하다
①the present-day consensus will secure the building's merit for good✕ 내 답
②a building's significance is fixed by its architect's original intent
③they can foresee the direction architecture will take✓ 정답
④the listing will raise the building's economic value at once
⑤future generations are bound to honour the judgments of the past
Why someone with your profile chose ①

You chose answer ① here. This is a classic distractor choice that tricks the reader by repeating words used throughout the passage, in this case present-day, consensus, and merit; the question writer is relying on the reader matching words instead of following the argument. This comes back to your inference. The correct answer, ③, needed you to connect three ideas: what people value keeps changing, a listing still claims the future, so the judgement is in the end the board's own. To avoid this in future, before you choose, ask whether an option is just repeating the passage's words. What is the relationship between the ideas, not the words?

Why someone with your profile lost marks here

Here you got the hard part right, the relative clause the painting the collector bought, which is the trickiest piece. The slips are small: a missing article (the or a), and become should be becomes. This is the writing to a word constraint issue from your diagnostic. On a multiple-choice question you would recognise the correct sentence, but producing it yourself is where the mistakes appear. To improve this, practise writing the sentence from scratch and check two things each time: did you include the article, and does the verb agree with the subject?
